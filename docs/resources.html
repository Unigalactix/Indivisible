01 / Ideas, decoded

The A–Z glossary.

Type to narrow the glossary.

AKS
The Agrawal–Kayal–Saxena test: an unconditional, deterministic algorithm that decides primality in time polynomial in the number of input digits.
Arithmetic progression
A sequence with a constant difference between successive terms. Dirichlet's and Green–Tao's theorems describe different ways primes occur in these sequences.
Asymptotic
Describes a limiting relationship as a parameter grows. f(x) ∼ g(x) means their ratio approaches 1, not that their difference approaches zero.
Carmichael number
A composite n satisfying an−1 ≡ 1 (mod n) for every integer a coprime to n. The smallest example is 561.
Composite
An integer greater than 1 with more than two positive divisors. For example, 12 = 3 × 4.
Coprime
Two integers whose greatest common divisor is 1. They need not individually be prime.
Divisor
An integer that divides another with no remainder. Our analyzer lists positive divisors, including 1 and the input itself.
Fermat's Little Theorem
For a prime p and any integer a, ap ≡ a (mod p). Do not confuse this with Fermat's Last Theorem.
Fermat number
A number of the form 22n + 1 for a nonnegative integer n. The first five are prime, but not all are.
Finite field
A finite arithmetic system with addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division by nonzero elements. Its size is always a prime power.
Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic
Every integer greater than 1 is a product of primes in exactly one way, apart from the order of the factors.
GIMPS
The Great Internet Mersenne Prime Search: a distributed computing project founded in 1996 to find Mersenne primes.
Goldbach conjecture
The strong form says every even integer at least 4 is the sum of two primes. It is unproved; the related odd three-prime statement is a theorem.
Greatest common divisor
The largest positive integer dividing both inputs, often calculated efficiently with Euclid's algorithm.
L-function
An analytic object encoding arithmetic information, often through a series and Euler product. Zeta and Dirichlet L-functions are examples.
Lucas-Lehmer test
A deterministic test for Mersenne numbers with prime exponents. For odd prime p, iterate s → s² − 2 starting at 4, modulo 2p − 1; a zero after p − 2 iterations proves primality.
Mersenne prime
A prime of the form 2p − 1. Examples include 3, 7, 31, and 127. A prime exponent is necessary but not sufficient.
Miller-Rabin
A primality test using modular exponentiation. Failed rounds prove compositeness; passing random rounds generally establishes probable primality, not a proof.
Modular arithmetic
Arithmetic with remainders. Writing a ≡ b (mod m) means a and b have the same remainder when divided by m.
Multiplicative inverse
A value b with ab ≡ 1 (mod m). It exists exactly when gcd(a, m) = 1.
p-adic number
A number in a completion based on divisibility by powers of a fixed prime p, rather than ordinary real absolute distance.
Perfect number
A positive integer equal to the sum of its positive proper divisors. Every even perfect number corresponds to a Mersenne prime.
Prime
A positive integer greater than 1 with exactly two positive divisors: 1 and itself.
Prime gap
The difference between consecutive primes. A gap of g has g − 1 integers strictly between its endpoints.
Prime Number Theorem
The number of primes at most x is asymptotic to x / ln(x): the ratio approaches 1 as x grows without bound.
Probable prime
An integer passing a specified test that every prime in the test's domain passes, but that some composites can also pass.
Pseudoprime
A composite that passes a particular probable-prime test. Always specify the test or base; being an impostor in one test does not mean passing them all.
Riemann Hypothesis
The unproved statement that every nontrivial zero of the Riemann zeta function has real part 1/2.
Safe prime
A prime q for which (q − 1)/2 is also prime. Not the same as a guarantee that any use of q is cryptographically safe.
Sieve of Eratosthenes
An algorithm that lists primes up to a limit by crossing out multiples of primes starting from their squares.
Sophie Germain prime
A prime p for which 2p + 1 is also prime.
Totient
Euler's φ(n), the count of integers from 1 through n coprime to n. If p is prime, φ(p) = p − 1.
Twin primes
Two primes differing by exactly 2, such as 17 and 19. Whether there are infinitely many pairs is still unknown.
Ulam spiral
An arrangement of integers on an outward square spiral, often with primes highlighted to expose visual patterns.
Unit
In the positive integers, 1. It is the multiplicative identity and is neither prime nor composite.
Zeta function
For real s > 1, ζ(s) = ∑ n−s. Its extension to complex inputs and its zeros reveal deep information about the distribution of primes.
