A 30-second refresher: primes, factors, and connections

A factor divides a number without a remainder: 3 is a factor of 12 because 12 ÷ 3 = 4. A prime is a whole number greater than 1 whose only positive factors are 1 and itself, such as 2, 3, 5, and 7. A composite can be made by multiplying smaller numbers greater than 1: 12 = 2 × 2 × 3. The number 1 is neither.

On this map, a “node” just means a circle, and an “edge” just means a line. In the default view, a line connects numbers one prime multiplication apart. For example, 3 connects to 6 (multiply by 2), and 6 connects to 12 (multiply by 2 again).

Rules, scoring, and the difference between play and proof

Each new prime or accepted factor earns 10 points; completing a round earns 100. Mistakes and hints cost 5 points, with a minimum score of zero. Path quests score only on completion: 100 minus penalties and 5 per move beyond the shortest route. Revisiting nodes cannot farm points. Re-selecting a collected prime or your current path node has no penalty.

The optional countdown pauses when you pause the game or leave the browser tab. Paused and ended rounds cannot accept moves. Starting again creates a fresh round; factor targets and path routes rotate. Scores stay in this tab only. These finite games teach definitions and relationships, not proofs of infinite conjectures.
