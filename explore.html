01 / Numbers at an extraordinary scale

The largest known primes.

The current top five are all Mersenne primes: one less than a power of two. Their special form makes efficient primality proofs possible with the Lucas-Lehmer test.

A cloud-sized discovery

Luke Durant's network spanned thousands of GPUs across 24 datacenter regions in 17 countries. An NVIDIA A100 in Dublin reported a probable prime on October 11, 2024; an H100 in San Antonio confirmed primality with Lucas-Lehmer on October 12.

Independent tests on different hardware and programs then verified the result.

A special form. Not a guarantee.

If 2p − 1 is prime, p must be prime. The reverse does not always hold: 211 − 1 = 2,047 = 23 × 89.

Other forms also produce enormous primes. The sixth-largest entry is the generalized Fermat prime 2,524,1902,097,152 + 1, with 13,426,224 digits, discovered in 2025.

02 / Different questions. Different tools.

How do we find a prime?

Listing every small prime and proving that one enormous number is prime are different jobs. Select a method to see how it works.

GENERATES PRIMESO(n log log n)

The Sieve of Eratosthenes

Think of sifting sand: remove what doesn't belong, and the primes remain. This method lists all primes up to a chosen limit.

  1. Write down the integers from 2 through n. Keep 1 separate: it is neither prime nor composite.
  2. Take the smallest uncrossed number p. It's prime. Cross out its multiples, starting at p²; smaller multiples were already handled.
  3. Repeat for uncrossed p up to √n. Every uncrossed number remaining is prime.

Why stop at √n? Every composite at most n has a prime factor at most √n.

TESTS ONE NUMBERUSUALLY PROBABILISTIC

The Miller-Rabin test

Think of a series of identity checks. A failed check exposes a composite. Passing many checks makes a number a strong probable-prime candidate.

  1. Handle 2, 3, and even numbers first. For odd n > 3, write n − 1 = 2sd with d odd.
  2. Choose a base a from 2 through n − 2 and calculate ad mod n. If the result is 1 or n − 1, this round passes.
  3. Otherwise, square modulo n up to s − 1 times. The round passes if a result is n − 1. If none is, n is composite.

For a fixed odd composite, k independently and uniformly chosen random bases have a false-pass probability at most 4−k. That is not the posterior probability that a passing number is composite.

Carefully chosen fixed bases give deterministic results within specified integer bounds. Outside those bounds, passing rounds alone is not a proof.
PROVES PRIMALITYDETERMINISTIC POLYNOMIAL TIME

The AKS primality test

Agrawal, Kayal, and Saxena's 2002 breakthrough proved that primality can be decided deterministically in polynomial time without unproved hypotheses.

Its starting idea resembles Fermat's theorem for polynomials: for prime n, (x + a)n ≡ xn + a (mod n). AKS combines perfect-power and factor checks with carefully bounded polynomial congruences modulo xr − 1.

Polynomial in what? The number of digits of n, not n itself. This matters enormously in theory, although Miller-Rabin and specialized tests are generally more practical.

03 / The spaces in between

Close neighbors.
Vast distances.

A prime gap is the difference between consecutive primes. The primes are not random, but their local spacing is irregular. Near a large x, the average spacing is approximately ln(x).

01

The smallest gap

The gap between 2 and 3 is 1. It happens exactly once. Since 2 is the only even prime, all later primes are odd, so all later gaps are even and at least 2.

∞

No largest possible gap

For any n ≥ 2, the integers n! + 2, n! + 3, …, n! + n are all composite. Each n! + k is divisible by k. That gives n − 1 consecutive composites, as long a run as you like.

Largest known gaps with identified endpoints

  • Proven endpoints: 1,113,106. The primes at either end each have 18,662 digits. Found by P. Cami, M. Jansen, and J. K. Andersen.
  • Probable-prime endpoints: 16,045,848. Found by Andreas Höglund in March 2024, with endpoints of 385,713 digits. These endpoints passed strong tests but lack a final primality proof.

These are computational records with identified endpoints, not upper bounds on how big a gap can be.

A hundred numbers. One prime.

370,261 and 370,373 are consecutive primes. Their gap is 112, containing 111 composite integers strictly between them.

  • 370,261–370,360: 100 integers, one prime (370,261).
  • 370,262–370,361: 100 integers, zero primes.

These are sliding windows, not blocks aligned to multiples of 100. In the smaller window 91–100, only 97 is prime.

And yet, primes stay close.

The Twin Prime Conjecture says there are infinitely many prime pairs exactly 2 apart, such as 11 and 13. It remains unproved.

  • 2013: Yitang Zhang proves infinitely many pairs have gaps at most 70 million.
  • 2013–2014: Maynard and Tao develop stronger methods; the Polymath collaboration lowers the unconditional bound to 246.

A finite bound is a breakthrough. It is not yet a proof of the bound 2.
