THE ZETA FUNCTION

Can you hear the primes?

Think of prime counting as a sound: a smooth main tone, plus intricate overtones. The Riemann Zeta Function connects the counting numbers to the primes.

ζ(s) = 1 + 1/2s + 1/3s + …

For real s > 1, this sum equals a product over every prime:

ζ(s) = ∏p prime (1 − p−s)−1

Why? Expand each factor as a geometric series. Unique prime factorization makes every positive integer appear exactly once. This is Euler's product.

Extend the function to complex inputs (except its pole at 1), and its nontrivial zeros contribute oscillating corrections to smooth estimates of prime counts. Like harmonics shaping a sound, these corrections reveal fine structure.

The analogy has limits. Primes are not literally musical notes, and the series above does not converge everywhere. The complex extension is what lets mathematicians study the zeros.
MODULAR ARITHMETIC & FERMAT
01

Math that goes full circle.

It's 10 o'clock. Four hours later it's 2, not 14. You've already used modular arithmetic: keep the remainder after dividing by a fixed number.

14 ≡ 2 (mod 12)

Fermat's Little Theorem says that for a prime p and any integer a:

ap ≡ a (mod p)

If a is not divisible by p, an equivalent form is ap−1 ≡ 1 (mod p). On a 7-position clock, 36 = 729 = 104 × 7 + 1: you land on 1.

That reliable pattern helps test primality. Related modular arithmetic also explains part of how RSA encryption works.

A warning about impostors. 341 = 11 × 31 is composite, yet 2340 ≡ 1 (mod 341). Passing a Fermat test alone does not prove primality. Miller-Rabin asks stronger questions.

A conjecture is a statement mathematicians believe may be true, but have not proved. Even overwhelming numerical evidence cannot replace a proof about infinitely many cases.

OPEN PROBLEM

The Riemann Hypothesis

Every nontrivial zero of the zeta function has real part exactly 1/2. If true, this would give exceptionally sharp control over the error in estimates of prime counts.

The connection between zeros and primes is established mathematics. Their proposed alignment on this “critical line” is the unproved part.

OPEN PROBLEM

The Twin Prime Conjecture

Are there infinitely many primes p such that p + 2 is also prime? We know infinitely many prime gaps are at most 246. We don't yet know whether infinitely many equal 2.

Arbitrarily large gaps and infinitely many bounded gaps can coexist. One describes how far primes can drift apart; the other describes how often they return close together.
