01 / The atoms of arithmetic

Some numbers can't
be broken down.

A divisor is a positive integer that divides a number without leaving a remainder. Count those divisors, and you can tell what kind of number you're looking at.

07

Prime

A positive integer greater than 1 with exactly two positive divisors: 1 and itself. The divisors of 7 are just 1 and 7.

12

Composite

An integer greater than 1 with more than two positive divisors. The divisors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, and 12.

01

The unit

1 has exactly one positive divisor: itself. It is neither prime nor composite. Zero and negative integers are not primes in this setting either.

Why “atoms”? Every integer greater than 1 has a unique prime factorization, apart from the order of the factors. For example, 84 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 7. This is the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic.

02 / A very human story

Ancient questions.
Extraordinary discoveries.

c. 300 BC

Euclid looks beyond infinity

Euclid proves that no finite list can contain every prime. Multiply the primes on a list and add 1: some prime divisor of that new number is missing from the list. The new number need not itself be prime.

3rd century BC

Eratosthenes makes a sieve

Rather than test each number separately, systematically cross out multiples of primes. A method attributed to Eratosthenes still makes a remarkably good introduction to algorithms today.

1644

Mersenne explores powers of two

Marin Mersenne studies numbers of the form 2p − 1. His proposed list of prime-producing exponents contains mistakes, but the numbers now bear his name.

1732

Euler breaks a convincing pattern

Fermat suspected every number of the form 22n + 1 was prime. Euler shows that 232 + 1 = 641 × 6,700,417. A few successful examples are not a proof.

1896

The Prime Number Theorem

Hadamard and de la Vallée-Poussin independently prove that the count of primes up to x is asymptotic to x / ln(x). Around a large x, prime density is roughly 1 / ln(x).

1996

A global search begins

George Woltman starts the Great Internet Mersenne Prime Search: GIMPS. Volunteers contribute computing power to a search far bigger than any one computer.

October 12, 2024

41 million digits. One new prime.

Luke Durant's GIMPS search confirms 2136,279,841 − 1, with 41,024,320 decimal digits. His network uses thousands of server GPUs spanning 24 datacenter regions in 17 countries.

October 7, 2026

The search continues

The 2024 discovery remains the largest known prime in the PrimePages list checked on this date. There are infinitely many primes, but only finitely many that we have identified.

03 / Get to know the neighbors

1,000 integers.
168 primes.

Explore every prime from 1 to 1,000, grouped into blocks of 100. Open a range to see its numbers. The list is generated by the same sieve principle you can try in the lab.

A small surprise: the block from 91 to 100 has just one prime: 97. Primes are predictable in their overall density, but not evenly spaced.
